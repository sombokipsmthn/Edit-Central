# Talking-Head Editing Master

**Purpose:** Turn long or loosely structured talking-head recordings into a coherent, natural, watchable edit without losing the speaker's meaning, personality, credibility, or emotional intent.

## When to Use

- Interviews and documentary contributor edits
- Talking-head YouTube videos and educational explainers
- Presentations, tutorials, and recorded lessons
- Testimonials, case studies, and corporate communications
- Podcast video and multi-camera conversations
- Any project where spoken content is the primary narrative

## Instructions

### 1. Understand the Project

1. Identify the audience, platform, duration, tone, and purpose
2. Establish the central message and what the viewer should understand or do
3. Confirm aspect ratio, frame rate, resolution, audio requirements, and delivery format
4. Identify required statements, legal or factual sensitivities, approved terminology, and material that must not be altered
5. Review the available footage, audio, B-roll, graphics, and references
6. Preserve the original media and create a safe working project or sequence

**Do not begin by cutting every pause. First understand what the speaker is communicating and how the material should be structured.**

### 2. Prepare and Organise Media

1. Separate camera footage, external audio, B-roll, music, graphics, and other assets into clearly named groups or bins
2. Check that source files are online and play correctly
3. Confirm that video and external audio belong together
4. Synchronise separately recorded audio where required. Waveform synchronisation can help when recordings contain a clear common sound; verify sync by listening and checking visible mouth movement
5. For multiple cameras, identify the angles and confirm that recordings are synchronised before multicamera editing
6. Label useful takes, speakers, scenes, and important moments where this improves navigation
7. Keep original media and an untouched source sequence available

**Do not assume automatic synchronisation is correct. Check the beginning, middle, and end of long recordings for drift.**

### 3. Review the Complete Recording

Watch or listen to the full recording before detailed editing.

**Identify:**
- The strongest explanation of the main idea
- Important facts, examples, and supporting statements
- Strong opening and closing moments
- Natural pauses, emphasis, humour, emotion, and changes in energy
- Repeated explanations and unnecessary detours
- Mistakes, false starts, interruptions, and unusable sections
- B-roll opportunities and visual references
- Statements whose meaning depends on surrounding context

Create a paper edit, selects list, transcript annotation, or timecoded outline. Keep source timecodes so selected material can be found again.

**A transcript helps locate content, but it is not a substitute for reviewing the recording. Transcripts can misrecognise words, omit non-verbal information, and fail to communicate delivery, tone, or emotion.**

### 4. Build the Narrative Edit

1. Establish a clear structure: introduction, logical development of the main points, and conclusion
2. Select the strongest complete thoughts rather than automatically choosing the shortest takes
3. Remove material that is genuinely irrelevant, repetitive, confusing, or distracting
4. Reorder sections only when the new order preserves meaning and does not create misleading implications
5. Use an opening that quickly establishes the subject or gives viewers a meaningful reason to continue
6. Build transitions between ideas so the edit feels like one connected explanation
7. Retain enough context for every statement to remain accurate and understandable
8. Record substantial rearrangements or omissions when editorial accountability matters

**Do not construct a sentence from disconnected fragments if the result changes the speaker's meaning, certainty, tone, or intent. Do not remove qualifications merely to make a statement sound stronger.**

### 5. Refine Dialogue and Pacing

1. Remove obvious false starts, accidental repetitions, unusable takes, and distracting verbal mistakes when they do not contribute to the speaker's character or meaning
2. Shorten long pauses when they interrupt the flow, but retain pauses that communicate thought, emotion, emphasis, or natural conversational rhythm
3. Remove filler words selectively. Keep them when they are part of the speaker's natural delivery or when removing them would sound unnatural
4. Listen across every dialogue cut. Check room tone, breath, vocal level, background noise, and cadence
5. Use audio overlaps, room tone, or supporting visuals where appropriate to make picture edits feel less abrupt
6. Avoid cutting so tightly that the speaker appears rushed, robotic, or unable to breathe
7. Review at normal playback speed. Do not judge pacing only by waveform or transcript

**The goal is clarity and flow, not the maximum possible number of cuts. Preserve the speaker's personality and performance.**

### 6. Improve Visual Flow and Coverage

1. Use the primary camera angle when expression, credibility, or direct address is important
2. Use alternate angles, reframing, or punch-ins when they improve emphasis or conceal an unavoidable visual edit
3. Add B-roll when it illustrates, contextualises, or supports the spoken point
4. Use graphics, captions, screenshots, or diagrams when they make information easier to understand
5. Keep visual changes motivated by story or information
6. Check that B-roll does not imply something the speaker did not say or show an event inaccurately
7. Maintain continuity of screen direction, eyelines, framing, and visual scale where relevant
8. Check captions and on-screen text for spelling, timing, legibility, and safe placement

**When no relevant visual coverage exists, a clean talking-head shot is often better than unrelated stock footage.**

### 7. Improve Audio Continuity

1. Make dialogue consistently intelligible before balancing music
2. Compare dialogue levels across edits and recording setups
3. Use clip gain, track automation, compression, equalisation, noise reduction, or other processing only when needed
4. Preserve natural room tone and ambience so edits do not create distracting background changes
5. Use fades or crossfades to smooth audio transitions where necessary
6. Keep music below dialogue and automate it so important words remain clear
7. Listen for clipping, distortion, hum, hiss, plosives, excessive sibilance, abrupt noise changes, and missing audio
8. Check the mix on more than one playback system when possible

**There is no single dialogue level, compressor setting, or loudness target for every project. Follow the delivery specification and the source recording. Avoid aggressive processing that makes the voice sound unnatural.**

### 8. Review the Complete Edit

Watch the entire sequence from beginning to end without skipping around.

**Check that:**
- The opening establishes the subject quickly and accurately
- The narrative is understandable without the editor's explanation
- Every statement retains its intended meaning and context
- Cuts feel deliberate and dialogue remains natural
- Pauses and breaths sound appropriate
- B-roll and graphics support the spoken content
- Audio remains consistent and intelligible
- Captions and graphics are accurate and readable
- There are no black frames, offline media, accidental gaps, flash frames, or unintended overlaps
- The ending feels complete rather than abruptly truncated

Make targeted corrections and review affected sections again. If a change affects the narrative or pacing more broadly, review the complete sequence again.

## Hook Preservation

**Maintain the strongest original hook and its intended message throughout editing. Do not weaken or rewrite the opening unless structural analysis reveals it as unclear.**

## Punch-In Guidance

Use restrained punch-ins:
- Generally around 110–115% scale
- With smooth easing curves
- For visual continuity, not distraction
- Avoid excessive or distracting zooms

## B-Roll Sourcing

The agent may identify and mark B-roll opportunities, but:
- **Must not independently source or insert unapproved stock footage**
- Follow the project's sourcing instructions
- When coverage is insufficient, retain a clean talking-head shot

## AI-Assisted Editing

When an AI assistant has access to an editing project:

1. Inspect the active project and sequence before proposing changes
2. Identify available media, sequence settings, track layout, and relevant source clips
3. Establish the intended audience, story, duration, and delivery requirements
4. Review transcripts and metadata, but do not treat them as definitive evidence of performance or meaning
5. Propose a paper edit or timecoded structure before substantial changes
6. Preserve the original sequence. Duplicate it or create a clearly named working version before destructive or extensive edits
7. Make changes in manageable stages: assembly, dialogue refinement, visual coverage, audio continuity, and final review
8. After each stage, inspect the timeline and play back affected sections
9. Do not claim an edit is complete unless the tool confirms the operation and the result has been checked
10. Report completed actions, assumptions, unresolved issues, and anything requiring human judgement

**If the assistant cannot inspect or control the editing application, it should provide an edit plan, timecoded instructions, or checklist rather than claiming to have changed the project.**

## Application Notes

### Premiere Pro Specific Procedures

**Project Organisation:**
```
PROJECT_NAME/
├── Project_Files/
├── Footage/
├── Audio/
├── Graphics/
├── Sequences/
│   ├── Scratch/
│   └── Final/
├── Exports/
└── References/
```

**Scratch → Final Workflow:**
1. Create and edit the initial sequence in Scratch
2. Complete the rough edit
3. Save the Premiere Pro project
4. Duplicate the Scratch sequence
5. Move or organise the duplicate into Final
6. Rename the duplicate `Final Sequence`
7. Preserve the original Scratch sequence unchanged
8. Perform subsequent refinement on Final Sequence

**Editing Tools:**
- Razor tool (C) for cuts
- Ripple delete (Shift+Delete) for removal
- J-cuts: Lead incoming audio 3-4 frames early
- Essential Sound panel for dialogue processing

### DaVinci Resolve Specific Procedures

- Media Page: Import and organise clips
- Cut Page: Initial assembly
- Edit Page: Detailed editing
- Fusion Page: Graphics and composites
- Colour Page: Primary and creative grading
- Fairlight Page: Audio finishing
- Deliver Page: Export configuration only (no actual export)

## Required Inputs

- Raw talking-head footage
- Understanding of content message
- Timeline in Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve
- Access to ripple delete/insert tools

## Expected Outputs

- Refined sequence with natural flow
- Removed dead air and fillers
- Preserved sentence integrity
- Engaging pacing for platform
- Clean timing for captions
- Audio mix meeting -14 LUFS standard

## Quality Standards

- Viewer cannot identify removed content
- Natural speaking rhythm maintained
- No obvious jump cuts
- Captions will read cleanly
- Original meaning preserved
- Dialogue peaks between -6 dBFS and -3 dBFS
- Integrated mix at -14 LUFS

## Constraints and Limitations

- Do not make speech sound robotic
- Preserve speaker's authentic voice
- Don't change meaning of content
- Maintain original emotional delivery
- Do not remove all pauses - some are necessary for meaning
- Do not export or render the final deliverable (see prohibition below)
